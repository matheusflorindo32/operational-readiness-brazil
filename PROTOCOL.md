# Protocol v1.0 — FROZEN

**Freeze date:** 2026-08-25

## Research question
Which evidence-supported components, indicators, and implementation strategies can inform a feasible framework for assessing, developing, and maintaining operational readiness among Brazilian public safety professionals?

## Eligibility summary
Include adult operational public-safety populations and occupational trainees; comparable law-enforcement, firefighting and first-responder studies; military evidence only through the transferability rule. Include empirical studies, evidence syntheses, guidelines/consensus and prespecified official grey literature. Exclude sports-only populations, non-operational clinical evidence without readiness relevance, marketing/blog material and untraceable sources.

## Public safety scope
Core Brazilian target groups include PF, PRF, Civil Police, Military Police, Military Fire Brigades, Penal Police and operational Municipal Guards when eligible. Other SUSP-linked groups require explicit task/readiness comparability. Armed Forces are not the Brazilian target population.

## APHT rule
APHT/TEMS/TCCC/TECC evidence is eligible only when it informs medical readiness, operational competence, self/buddy care, hemorrhage control, casualty movement/extraction or performance under operational stress/load. Pure clinical trauma treatment without readiness relevance is excluded.

## Search architecture
Three independent families are executed per database/platform and deduplicated:
1. Core human performance/readiness.
2. APHT/medical readiness.
3. Implementation science.

No lower date limit, no language restriction, no geographic filter for international databases, and no NOT operator in the primary strategy.

## Databases/platforms
PubMed/MEDLINE; Scopus; Web of Science Core Collection; SPORTDiscus; CINAHL Complete; APA PsycINFO; Embase.com; Cochrane Library; SciELO; LILACS/BVS.

## Seed sensitivity
Prespecified pilot: 15/15 seed studies covered after adding independent APHT and implementation families. This is a seed-set sensitivity check, not an estimate of absolute review sensitivity.

## Transferability rule
International/military studies are assessed across seven dimensions: task, equipment/load, environment/exposure, construct/outcome, population, intervention feasibility and institutional compatibility. The score is normalized to 0–10: high 8–10, conditional 5–7, low 0–4. A highly specific military combat study never independently justifies a Brazilian institutional recommendation.

## Implementation mapping
Evidence → relevance/quality → transferability → CFIR determinants → Proctor implementation outcomes → resources/responsible actor/indicator → implementation horizon (0–30; 31–90; 3–12; >12 months).

## Reporting
JBI methodology; PRISMA-ScR primary reporting guideline; PRISMA-S for searches.

## Amendments
All post-freeze changes must be recorded in `AMENDMENTS.md` and `CHANGELOG.md` before they are used in analysis.
