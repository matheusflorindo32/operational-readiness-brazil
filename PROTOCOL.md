## Study design

This project is a **conceptual and applied scientific essay informed by structured and reproducible evidence identification, critical synthesis, Brazilian contextual analysis, transferability assessment, and implementation-science concepts**.

It is **not** a systematic review, scoping review, meta-analysis, or experimental study.

The earlier scoping-review framing was a pre-registration design draft and was superseded before definitive evidence identification. This change is documented in `AMENDMENTS.md` and `CHANGELOG.md` rather than silently erased from the project history.

## Central proposition

The tactical athlete construct should serve as a conceptual starting point, rather than an endpoint, for Brazilian public safety. Operational readiness is treated as a multidimensional and longitudinal construct integrating health, physical capability, occupational performance, cognition, sleep/recovery, occupational load, injury prevention, medical readiness, monitoring, organizational conditions, and occupational longevity.

> **Atleta tático é o ponto de partida, não o ponto final.**

## Paradigm-shift rationale

The essay addresses an emerging need for a paradigm shift in attention to Brazilian public-safety personnel: from fragmented, episodic, and predominantly physical approaches toward an integrated, longitudinal, multidimensional, and implementation-oriented model of operational readiness, health, sustainable human performance, and occupational longevity.

## Primary research question

**Português**  
Quais componentes, indicadores e estratégias de implementação, sustentados pelas evidências disponíveis, podem compor um framework factível para avaliar, desenvolver e manter a prontidão operacional de profissionais da segurança pública no Brasil?

**English**  
Which evidence-supported components, indicators, and implementation strategies can inform a feasible framework for assessing, developing, and maintaining operational readiness among Brazilian public safety professionals?

## General objective

Develop an evidence-informed applied framework for assessing, developing, and maintaining operational readiness in Brazilian public safety by critically integrating tactical-athlete concepts, human-performance evidence, Brazilian public-safety evidence, transferability considerations, and implementation-science principles.

## Brazilian public-safety scope

The core target includes operational personnel from Federal Police, Federal Highway Police, Civil Police, Military Police, Military Fire Brigades, Penal Police, and eligible operational Municipal Guards. Other SUSP-linked groups require explicit task/readiness comparability. Brazilian Armed Forces are evidence-transfer sources, not the target population.

## Structured evidence identification

Evidence will be identified through a structured and reproducible strategy intended to support the conceptual essay. It does not claim the exhaustive completeness of a systematic review.

Three complementary evidence families are retained:
1. **Core human performance / operational readiness**.
2. **APHT / medical readiness**.
3. **Implementation science**.

Planned sources include PubMed/MEDLINE; Scopus; Web of Science Core Collection; SPORTDiscus; CINAHL Complete; APA PsycINFO; Embase; Cochrane Library; SciELO; LILACS/BVS; Brazilian official/grey literature; and citation chasing when appropriate.

No production result counts will be reported until the definitive evidence-identification phase is actually executed.

## Seed-set pilot

A prespecified set of 15 seed studies spanning operational readiness, Brazilian public safety, wearables, APHT, and implementation science was used to test **conceptual coverage** of the evidence-identification architecture. The initial core family covered 12/15 seeds; dedicated APHT and implementation families were added, after which 15/15 were conceptually covered.

This result is a **seed-set coverage check**, not an estimate of absolute search sensitivity.

## APHT / tactical medicine

APHT/TEMS/TCCC/TECC evidence may inform the essay when directly related to medical readiness, self/buddy care, hemorrhage control, casualty movement/extraction, operational competence, or performance under stress, fatigue, or load. Purely clinical trauma-treatment evidence without operational-readiness relevance is outside the conceptual core.

## Transferability

Military and other tactical evidence is not treated as automatically equivalent to Brazilian public safety. Transferability will be critically assessed across:
1. Task/demand similarity.
2. Equipment/load similarity.
3. Environment/exposure similarity.
4. Construct/outcome relevance.
5. Population comparability.
6. Feasibility of assessment/intervention.
7. Legal, ethical, and institutional compatibility.

Highly combat-specific military evidence will never independently justify an institutional recommendation for Brazilian public safety.

## Implementation science

Implementation considerations will be informed by internationally established implementation-science frameworks:
- **CFIR** for determinants and context.
- **Proctor Implementation Outcomes** for acceptability, appropriateness, feasibility, adoption, fidelity, cost, reach/penetration, and sustainability.
- **ERIC** only as a subsidiary taxonomy for naming implementation strategies when evidence supports their use.

These frameworks are analytical supports, not universal mandatory research protocols.

## Expected product

The intended product is a **proposed evidence-informed Brazilian Operational Readiness Framework**. It will not be described as validated unless subsequent empirical validation is conducted. Future validation studies and possible digital operational-readiness tools may constitute separate research phases.

## Transparency and amendments

Any substantive post-registration change must be prospectively documented in `AMENDMENTS.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`, including date, rationale, scope, and expected impact. The OSF-registered Conceptual Essay Protocol v1.0 remains the historical public baseline.
