## Abstract — pre-review scaffold

This manuscript is being developed as an evidence-informed conceptual and applied framework for operational readiness in tactical populations, with planned transfer to Brazilian public-safety settings. The present pre-review version contains only claims supported by the currently deep-qualified evidence set and explicitly marks domains where evidence remains insufficient. Human scientific adjudication has not yet been performed.

## 1. Introduction

Operational readiness is broader than generic physical fitness. In high-demand occupations, readiness requires the capacity to perform job-specific tasks, recover from injury, operate under environmental and logistical constraints, and interface effectively with medical and organizational systems. A firefighter return-to-work screening study illustrates the value of task-specific functional assessment: eight occupational tasks demonstrated generally good-to-excellent inter- and intra-rater reliability, although reliability alone does not establish predictive validity or fitness-for-duty effectiveness [EV-0643].

Within tactical medicine, operational readiness also depends on the interaction between personnel capability, equipment, training and system integration. Swedish tactical police medics described a mismatch between trained competencies and tasks actually performed, together with barriers at the police–civilian prehospital interface [EV-0103]. In a naval special-warfare unit, standardized commercial tourniquets were faster and had lower application-failure rates than an improvised comparator under simulated testing [EV-1103]. These findings support a multidimensional view of readiness but do not independently prove clinical outcome benefits.

## 2. Working conceptual architecture

The working framework treats operational readiness as a layered construct: individual capability, task demands, equipment/external load, environmental stressors, medical readiness, recovery/return-to-function, organizational implementation, and the operational consequence of performance failure.

Major current evidence gaps remain in cardiorespiratory fitness, strength/power, body composition, load carriage, sleep/fatigue, cognitive performance, nutrition/hydration and Brazilian validation.

## 3. Functional readiness and return to work

EV-0643 supports reproducibility of a job-specific firefighter return-to-work assessment but does not establish predictive validity, injury prevention or successful long-term return to duty. EV-0465 contributes indirect rehabilitation and physical-activity evidence from veterans with lower-limb amputation; it must not be generalized to healthy active tactical personnel.

## 4. Tactical medicine, TCCC/TECC and austere care

EV-1103 provides direct special-operations tourniquet performance data under simulated conditions. EV-0387 provides current narrative context on tourniquet use and training. EV-0394 supplies consensus-based austere critical-care guidance. EV-0224 supplies technology context for battlefield POCUS. EV-0946 illustrates tourniquet complications and reassessment needs but is insufficient to establish a universal causal 3-hour threshold.

## 5. Implementation science and systems readiness

EV-0503 and EV-0588 support a multilevel implementation perspective involving leadership, data sharing, staffing, policy, training, equipment and organizational culture. These studies describe barriers/facilitators and do not establish intervention effectiveness.

## 6. Brazil transferability

The current evidence base is predominantly international. Transfer to Brazilian public safety requires explicit treatment of police organization, SUS/SAMU interfaces, occupational regulation, equipment availability, training standards, climate, workload, regional heterogeneity and institutional governance.
